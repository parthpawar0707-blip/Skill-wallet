Context & Objectives

Understanding the Collegiate Well-Being Challenge

Traditional student mental health support is often sought reactively after severe distress or academic crisis. This project builds an empirical decision-support model to identify actionable lifestyle patterns for timely institutional support.

Academic & Environmental Pressures

Undergraduates navigate rigorous examination cycles, GPA benchmarking, and irregular schedules that compound emotional vulnerability and contribute to burnout.

Lifestyle Telemetry Correlation

Well-being does not deteriorate in isolation. Daily lifestyle indicators—cumulative screen hours, sleep quality, and physical activity—provide quantifiable baseline signals.

Proactive Decision Support

Provides academic mentors, residence directors, and counseling centers with visual cohort segmentation to design non-invasive wellness programs before acute crisis emerges.

Educational Research Notice: This analytics portfolio represents an educational business intelligence study designed for exploratory cohort modeling. It is not an automated medical diagnostic tool or clinical treatment recommendation system.

Empirical Insights

Key Analytical Discoveries & Implications

Synthesized from statistically verified patterns across the 200 student dataset in Tableau.

Sleep Quality as Primary Buffer

Students with Good sleep quality almost never experience high stress (only 2 of 45 students). Conversely, 53% of students with poor sleep suffer severe stress. Restorative sleep is the strongest inverse correlate of distress.

Digital Fatigue Threshold (7.5+ Hours)

Daily screen time escalates from 6.00 hours in Low stress to 8.12 hours in High stress. Exceeding 7.5 hours strongly co-occurs with clinical anxiety spikes, highlighting digital hygiene as an actionable intervention area.

Structured Interventions Proven Efficacy

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy achieves the highest measurable progress score (40.80), followed by Counseling (34.43), proving the tangible value of structured institutional care compared to unguided coping.

Correlation vs. Causation Notice: These findings represent observable statistical co-occurrences in the surveyed cohort. They highlight institutional intervention priorities rather than proving individual clinical causality.
