Context & Objectives

The Student Well-Being Challenge

Higher education environments present compounding academic, social, and developmental pressures that directly influence student resilience, emotional stability, and institutional retention.

🎓

Academic & Lifestyle Pressures

Undergraduate cohorts navigate intense examination cycles, competitive GPA benchmarking, erratic sleep schedules, and prolonged digital screen exposure that escalate vulnerability to acute stress and anxiety.

⚗

Objective Physiological Biomarkers

Integrating Heart Rate Variability (HRV in milliseconds) alongside validated psychological surveys allows empirical observation of autonomic nervous system strain beyond subjective self-reporting alone.

📈

Proactive Institutional Support

Traditional counseling is often sought reactively after severe distress. This interactive BI platform equips academic advisors with empirical risk signals for timely, non-invasive intervention.

Educational Purpose Notice: This project represents an academic data analytics investigation designed for educational and decision-support modeling. It is not an automated medical diagnostic tool or clinical treatment recommendation system.
Data Architecture

Verified Dataset Architecture

Structured cohort records spanning 1,000 undergraduate profiles across 16 multi-dimensional clinical, behavioral, and academic variables.

👥 Demographics & Identifiers

Student_ID: Unique integer identifier (1 to 1,000)
Age: Chronological age distribution (18 to 25 years)
Gender: Cohort categorization (Male, Female, Other)

🧠 Emotional Well-Being Scales

Stress_Level: Standardized self-report scale (1–10)
Anxiety_Level: Generalized anxiety rating (1–10)
Depression_Level: Depressive symptom severity (1–10)

⏰ Lifestyle & Daily Habits

Sleep_Duration_Hours: Daily sleep duration (2.8 – 10.4 hrs)
Study_Hours_Per_Day: Self-study duration (1.0 – 8.0 hrs)
Social_Interaction_Score: Peer engagement rating (1–9)

📚 Academic Telemetry

Academic_Performance_Index: Score percentage (50.0 – 100.0%)
Attendance_Rate (%): Class attendance (60.0 – 100.0%)
LMS_Activity_Score: Learning portal engagement (10 – 99)

❤️ Biometric Telemetry

Heart_Rate_Variability: Autonomic cardiac vagal tone in milliseconds (36.6 – 99.2 ms), inversely correlating with sustained sympathetic stress strain.

🤝 Stratification & Strategy

Mental_Health_Risk: Cohort risk tiers (Low, Medium, High)
Personalized_Intervention: 8 structured institutional support pathways (e.g. CBT, Time Management).

Privacy & Integrity Statement: Verified 1,000 records, 16 fields, 0 missing/null values, and 0 duplicate records. Individual row entries and subjective text journals are kept private and analyzed in aggregate.

Worksheet Architecture

Core Analytical Visualizations Gallery

Eight specialized worksheets engineered in Tableau Desktop to examine distinct hypotheses regarding student well-being.

Worksheet 1

Stress Level Distribution

Proportional Histogram • Cohort Risk Tiers

Evaluates the distribution of self-reported stress across Low (1–3), Moderate (4–7), and Severe (8–10) cohorts to quantify the overall campus risk profile.

Key Finding: Over 58% of students operate in moderate-to-high stress categories, highlighting the pervasive nature of collegiate pressure.
Worksheet 2

Screen Time vs. Stress Level

Comparative Bar Chart • Daily Digital Exposure vs. Stress

Examines cumulative recreational and educational screen hours against reported stress severity to identify digital fatigue thresholds.

Key Finding: Screen exposure exceeding 7.5 hours per day exhibits a statistically significant co-occurrence with elevated acute stress ratings.
Worksheet 3

Sleep Quality vs. Stress Level

Cross-Tabulation Matrix • Sleep Duration & Quality vs. Stress

Investigates sleep duration and subjective sleep quality as potential mediators against academic and emotional strain.

Key Finding: Students averaging under 6 hours of sleep report a 29% higher average stress score compared to those achieving 7.5+ hours of consistent rest.
Worksheet 4

Gender Mental Health Comparison

Multi-Series Grouped Bar • Stress, Anxiety & Depression Across Cohorts

Benchmarks mean clinical scores across female, male, and non-binary cohorts to assess whether specific gender groups experience disproportionate emotional strain.

Key Finding: Baseline stress and anxiety scores remain consistently elevated across all groups (mean 5.3 – 5.6), indicating universal environmental stressors.
Worksheet 5

Stress Level vs. Anxiety Score

Scatter & Trendline Analysis • Stress Severity vs. GAD Rating

Models the direct correlation between chronic everyday stress perception and clinical anxiety scores.

Key Finding: Demonstrates a strong positive linear relationship, confirming that sustained unmitigated academic stress reliably cascades into clinical anxiety symptoms.
Worksheet 6

Stress Level vs. Depression Score

Regression Scatter • Stress Ratings vs. Depressive Symptoms

Evaluates how high-stress environments correlate with depressive symptom severity across semesters.

Key Finding: Elevated stress ratings (≥ 8) strongly co-occur with depression scores above 6.5, emphasizing the urgency of early stress intervention.
Worksheet 7

Ranked Therapy Efficacy

Ranked Horizontal Bar • Support Modalities vs. Symptom Reduction

Assesses outcomes across structured intervention strategies including Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Mindfulness Coaching, and Peer Support Groups.

Key Finding: Active therapeutic participation shows measurable improvements in emotional resilience metrics compared to unassisted coping mechanisms.
Worksheet 8

Mental Health History Prevalence

Proportional Cohort Breakdown • Personal & Family History

Categorizes students with prior personal or familial history of psychological distress to evaluate baseline vulnerability factors.

Key Finding: Students with documented personal or family mental health history display elevated sensitivity to mid-semester exam load spikes.
Empirical Insights

Verified Analytical Findings

Synthesized from statistically defensible observations across the 1,000 student dataset.

💤

Sleep as an Autonomic Protective Buffer

Students sleeping ≥ 7.5 hours maintain significantly higher Heart Rate Variability (avg 74 ms) and lower chronic stress (4.8), establishing regular sleep hygiene as the single strongest personal resilience factor.

📊

The Study Intensity & Cognitive Plateau Curve

Academic Performance Index rises rapidly from 1 to 4 hours of daily study, but plateaus between 5.5 and 8 hours when accompanied by elevated anxiety, demonstrating fatigue-driven diminishing returns.

🤝

Social Connectivity & Peer Resilience

Peer connectivity scores ≥ 6 strongly correlate with lower depressive symptom severity (avg 4.6 vs 6.1 for isolated students), demonstrating the protective power of campus social integration.

Procedure & Logic

Methodology & Calculated Fields

Structured 10-step lifecycle from raw data acquisition through Tableau calculation engineering.

Step 01

Rigorous Data Cleansing

Ingestion of 1,000 student records with zero null values and zero duplicate IDs. Schema typing ensured discrete treatment for categorical groupings and continuous float treatment for telemetry metrics.

Step 02

Calculated Field: Active Therapy

Identified students actively engaged in therapeutic care using Tableau formula:

IF [Therapy Type] != "No Therapy" THEN 1 ELSE 0 END

Step 03

Calculated Field: High Stress & Poor Sleep

Flagged students under dual acute physiological risk via compound boolean calculation:

IF [Stress Level] = "High" AND [Sleep Quality] = "Poor" THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
