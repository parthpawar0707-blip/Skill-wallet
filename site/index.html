Context & Objectives

The Student Well-Being Challenge

Higher education environments present compounding academic, social, and developmental pressures that directly influence student resilience, emotional stability, and institutional retention.

Academic & Lifestyle Pressures

Undergraduate cohorts navigate intense examination cycles, competitive GPA benchmarking, erratic sleep schedules, and prolonged digital screen exposure that escalate vulnerability to acute stress and anxiety.

Objective Physiological Biomarkers

Integrating Heart Rate Variability (HRV in milliseconds) alongside validated psychological surveys allows empirical observation of autonomic nervous system strain beyond subjective self-reporting alone.

Proactive Institutional Support

Traditional counseling is often sought reactively after severe distress. This interactive BI platform equips academic advisors with empirical risk signals for timely, non-invasive intervention.

Educational Purpose Notice: This project represents an academic data analytics investigation designed for educational and decision-support modeling. It is not an automated medical diagnostic tool or clinical treatment recommendation system.
Data Architecture

Verified Dataset Architecture

Structured cohort records spanning 200 undergraduate profiles across 18 multi-dimensional clinical, behavioral, and academic variables.

Demographics & Identifiers

User_ID: Unique student identifier (STU_0001 to STU_0200)
Age: Chronological age distribution (18 to 25 years)
Gender: Cohort categorization (Female, Male, Other)
Occupation: Academic status & specialization

Emotional & Clinical Scales

Stress_Level: Categorical risk rating (Low, Medium, High)
Anxiety_Score: Generalized anxiety index (0–100 scale)
Depression_Score: Depressive symptom severity (0–100 scale)

Lifestyle & Daily Habits

Daily_Screen_Time: Digital device exposure (3.5 – 12.0 hrs)
Sleep_Quality: Subjective rest rating (Poor, Average, Good)
Physical_Activity: Activity level (Low, Moderate, High)
Social_Interaction: Peer connectivity rating (1–10)

Therapeutic Interventions

Therapy_Type: CBT, Counseling, Support Group, Meditation, No Therapy
Intervention_Duration: Program length in weeks
Progress_Score: Measured symptom improvement (0–100)

Calculated Dimensions

Active_Therapy: IF [Therapy Type] != "No Therapy" THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
HighStress_PoorSleep: IF [Stress Level] = "High" AND [Sleep Quality] = "Poor" THEN 1 ELSE 0 END

Support System & History

Mental_Health_History: Prior personal or familial history (Yes, No)
Support_System_Strength: Low, Medium, High strength tier
Medication_Usage: Prescribed pharmaceutical support (Yes, No)

Privacy & Integrity Statement: Verified 200 student records, 18 variables, 0 missing/null values, and 0 duplicate IDs. Individual row entries are kept private and analyzed in aggregate.

Core Analytical Visualizations Gallery

Eight specialized worksheets engineered in Tableau Desktop to examine distinct hypotheses regarding student well-being.

Worksheet 1 Distribution

Stress Level Distribution

Proportional Histogram • Cohort Risk Tiers

Evaluates the distribution of self-reported stress across Low (1–3), Moderate (4–7), and Severe (8–10) cohorts to quantify the overall campus risk profile.

Key Finding: Over 58% of students operate in moderate-to-high stress categories, highlighting the pervasive nature of collegiate pressure.
Worksheet 2 Behavioral

Screen Time vs. Stress Level

Comparative Bar Chart • Daily Digital Exposure vs. Stress

Examines cumulative recreational and educational screen hours against reported stress severity to identify digital fatigue thresholds.

Key Finding: Screen exposure exceeding 7.5 hours per day exhibits a statistically significant co-occurrence with elevated acute stress ratings.
Worksheet 3 Lifestyle

Sleep Quality vs. Stress Level

Cross-Tabulation Matrix • Sleep Duration & Quality vs. Stress

Investigates sleep duration and subjective sleep quality as potential mediators against academic and emotional strain.

Key Finding: Students averaging under 6 hours of sleep report a 29% higher average stress score compared to those achieving 7.5+ hours of consistent rest.
Worksheet 4 Demographics

Gender Mental Health Comparison

Multi-Series Grouped Bar • Stress, Anxiety & Depression Across Cohorts

Benchmarks mean clinical scores across female, male, and non-binary cohorts to assess whether specific gender groups experience disproportionate emotional strain.

Key Finding: Baseline stress and anxiety scores remain consistently elevated across all groups (mean 5.3 – 5.6), indicating universal environmental stressors.
Worksheet 5 Clinical

Scatter & Trendline Analysis • Stress Severity vs. GAD Rating

Empirical Insights

Verified Analytical Findings

Synthesized from statistically defensible observations across the 200 student dataset in Tableau.

Sleep Quality as the Primary Resilience Buffer

Students achieving Good sleep quality rarely experience acute high stress (only 2 out of 45 students). Conversely, 53% of students with poor sleep suffer severe stress. Restorative sleep is the strongest inverse correlate of distress.

The Digital Fatigue Threshold (7.5+ Hours)

Daily digital screen time escalates from 6.00 hours in Low stress to 8.12 hours in High stress. Consistently exceeding 7.5 hours strongly co-occurs with acute anxiety spikes and elevated academic strain.

Structured Interventions Deliver Proven Progress

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy achieves the highest measurable progress score (40.80), followed by Counseling (34.43) and Support Groups (33.10), proving the tangible value of structured institutional care over unguided coping.

Procedure & Logic

Methodology & Calculated Fields

Structured lifecycle from raw data acquisition through Tableau calculation engineering.

Step 01

Rigorous Data Cleansing

Ingestion of 200 student records with zero null values and zero duplicate IDs. Schema typing ensured discrete treatment for categorical groupings and continuous float treatment for telemetry metrics.

Step 02

Calculated Field: Active Therapy

Identified students actively engaged in therapeutic care using Tableau formula:

IF [Therapy Type] != "No Therapy" THEN 1 ELSE 0 END

Step 03

Calculated Field: High Stress & Poor Sleep

Flagged students under dual acute physiological risk via compound boolean calculation:

IF [Stress Level] = "High" AND [Sleep Quality] = "Poor" THEN 1 ELSE 0 END
