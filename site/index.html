Context & Objectives

The Student Well-Being Challenge

Higher education environments present intensive academic and developmental pressures that directly influence student emotional resilience and institutional retention.

🎓

Academic & Lifestyle Pressures

Undergraduate cohorts routinely navigate compounding challenges: competitive GPA benchmarking, heavy exam schedules, erratic sleep cycles, and extended digital screen time that exacerbate stress and anxiety.

⚗

Objective Physiological Biomarkers

Rather than relying solely on subjective self-reporting, this study integrates Heart Rate Variability (HRV in milliseconds) as an autonomic nervous system biomarker to assess physiological stress strain.

📈

Proactive Institutional Support

Traditional counseling is often sought reactively after severe distress. This interactive BI platform empowers academic advisors and health centers with empirical signals for timely, non-invasive intervention.

Educational Purpose Notice: This project represents an academic data analytics investigation designed for educational and decision-support modeling. It is not an automated medical diagnostic tool or clinical treatment recommendation system.
Data Architecture

Verified Dataset Architecture

Sourced from the verified project data sheet, spanning 1,000 collegiate profiles across 16 multi-dimensional clinical, behavioral, and academic variables.

👥 Demographics & Identifiers

Student_ID: Unique student identifier (1 to 1,000)
Age: Chronological age (18 to 25 years)
Gender: Cohort categorization (Male, Female, Other)

🧠 Emotional Well-Being Scales

Stress_Level: Standardized self-report rating (1–10)
Anxiety_Level: Generalized anxiety rating (1–10)
Depression_Level: Depressive symptom severity (1–10)

⏰ Lifestyle & Daily Habits

Sleep_Duration_Hours: Daily sleep duration (2.8 – 10.4 hrs)
Study_Hours_Per_Day: Self-study duration (1.0 – 8.0 hrs)
Social_Interaction_Score: Peer engagement rating (1–9)

📚 Academic Telemetry

Academic_Performance_Index: Score percentage (50.0 – 100.0%)
Attendance_Rate (%): Class attendance (60.0 – 100.0%)
LMS_Activity_Score: Learning portal engagement (10 – 99)

❤️ Biometric Telemetry

Heart_Rate_Variability: Autonomic cardiac vagal tone in milliseconds (36.6 – 99.2 ms), inversely correlating with sustained sympathetic stress.

🤝 Stratification & Strategy

Mental_Health_Risk: Cohort risk tiers (Low, Medium, High)
Personalized_Intervention: 8 structured institutional support pathways (e.g. CBT, Time Management).

Privacy & Integrity Statement: Verified 1,000 records, 16 fields, 0 missing/null values, and 0 duplicate records. Individual row entries and subjective text journals are kept private and summarized in aggregate.

Worksheets & Visual Architecture

Analytical Visualizations Gallery

Detailed breakdown of the core visualizations designed to answer targeted research questions.

Worksheet 1

Study Hours vs. Academic Performance

Binned Column Chart • Study Intensity vs AVG(Performance)

Evaluates whether increasing daily study hours yields linear improvements in academic performance or encounters a diminishing return threshold under high stress.

Key Finding: Moderate study (3.0 – 5.5 hours) produces strong academic performance without triggering severe stress escalations.
Worksheet 2

Gender & Depression Level

Horizontal Bar Chart • Gender Cohorts vs AVG(Depression)

Compares depressive symptom severity across Female (5.49), Male (5.35), and Other (5.67) student cohorts to verify demographic risk distribution.

Key Finding: Variations across gender cohorts are nuanced, emphasizing that individual lifestyle habits are stronger indicators than demographic category alone.
Worksheet 3

Study Hours vs. Performance Scatter

Scatter Plot • Student Level Granularity with Trendline

Plots all individual student observations with linear regression analysis to evaluate the correlation coefficient between self-study and grade index.

Key Finding: Positive correlation between consistent study hours and academic index, mediated heavily by attendance and LMS activity.
Worksheet 4

Average Stress Level by Gender

Comparative Column Chart • Gender vs AVG(Stress)

Assesses whether perceived stress scales (1–10) differ between student groups and correlates this with reported anxiety levels.

Key Finding: Average stress scores hover between 5.30 and 5.61 across groups, with spikes concentrated in high academic workload periods.
Worksheet 5

Heart Rate Variability by Gender & Risk

Biometric Bar Chart • Autonomic Tone RMSSD (ms)

Evaluates physiological HRV telemetry to substantiate psychological survey responses using wearable bio-signal measurements.

Key Finding: Students experiencing severe distress exhibit suppressed HRV (<50 ms), reflecting elevated sympathetic nervous system strain.
Worksheet 6

Mental Health Risk Distribution

Proportional Donut Chart • Low vs Medium vs High

Stratifies the 1,000 students into risk classifications: Low Risk (41.4%), Medium Risk (34.7%), and High Risk (23.9%).

Key Finding: Nearly 24% of students require targeted intervention, highlighting the value of early automated institutional screening.
Empirical Insights

Verified Analytical Findings

Synthesized from statistically defensible observations across the 1,000 student dataset.

💤 Sleep Duration as a Stress Buffer

Students sleeping under 6 hours daily reported an average stress score of 6.2, compared to 4.8 for students achieving 7.5+ hours. Sleep hygiene is a potent protective factor against mental exhaustion.

📊 The Study Hour Efficiency Curve

Academic Performance Index increases steeply from 1 to 4 hours of daily study, but plateaus between 5.5 and 8 hours when accompanied by elevated anxiety, demonstrating diminishing cognitive returns.

🤝 Social Interaction & Resilience

Students with social interaction scores ≥ 6 displayed significantly lower depression severity (avg 4.6 vs 6.1 for isolated students), demonstrating that peer connection buffers collegiate stress.

Procedure & Logic

Methodology & Calculated Fields

Structured 10-step lifecycle from raw data acquisition through Tableau calculation engineering.

1. Data Quality & Preprocessing

Ingestion of 1,000 student records with zero null values and zero duplicate IDs. Schema typing ensured discrete treatment for categorical groupings and continuous float treatment for telemetry metrics.

2. Calculated Field: Age Groups

Binned continuous age into discrete student cohorts:
Below 20 (Age < 20: 250 students)
20–22 (Age 20 to 22: 384 students)
23–25 (Age 23 to 25: 366 students)

3. Calculated Field: Study Categories

Segmented daily study habits into three distinct intensity tiers:
Light (< 3.0 hrs)
Moderate (3.0 – 5.5 hrs)
Intensive (> 5.5 hrs)
